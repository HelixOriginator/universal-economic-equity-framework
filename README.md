## Overview

**The Economic Equity Framework** (`universal-economic-equity-framework`) is an interactive policy whitepaper and analytical web application examining a transition from category-based quotas to a unified, community-blind welfare architecture. Anchored in the **Universal Poverty Principle**, this framework evaluates how verified economic distress—irrespective of caste, creed, or community—can serve as the sole criterion for scholarships, public welfare schemes (*yojanas*), and institutional upliftment.

---

## Key Conceptual Modules

### 1. Foundational Premise: The Universal Poverty Principle
* **The Merit–Livelihood Paradox:** Addresses the structural gap faced by independent researchers, innovators, and professionals who achieve significant intellectual milestones yet experience acute financial precarity without institutional safety nets.
* **Resolving the "Creamy Layer" & Regulatory Friction:** Demonstrates how real-time economic verification automatically graduates affluent households out of state subsidies, redirecting resources toward the genuinely destitute while reducing regulatory friction in higher education.

### 2. Comparative Governance Matrix
Provides an interactive side-by-side and focused comparison between:
* **Economic Vulnerability & Universal Welfare Model:** Need-based, community-blind support designed to eradicate material poverty directly and foster social cohesion.
* **Historical Redress & Institutional Representation Model:** Constitutional rationale under Articles 15 and 16, anti-discrimination safeguards, and the 103rd Constitutional Amendment (EWS) precedent.

### 3. Constitutional Jurisprudence & 15-Year Demographic Dynamics
* **Open-Pool Equilibrium:** Examines the structural interdependence between the unreserved open-competition pool and category-based reservations alongside landmark Supreme Court precedents (*Indra Sawhney*, *Saurav Yadav*, and *Janhit Abhiyan*).
* **Demographic & Talent Horizon:** Analyzes long-term demographic projections, empirical population baselines, and the economic risks of high-skilled emigration ("brain drain") when unreserved talent experiences sustained institutional exclusion.

### 4. Interactive Economic Vulnerability Index (EVI) Simulator
A client-side policy simulator allowing researchers, policymakers, and citizens to test household eligibility across three tiers:
* **Tier 1 (Priority Upliftment):** 100% tuition grants, livelihood stipends, and priority *yojana* access.
* **Tier 2 (Transitional Support):** Partial fee subsidies and targeted skill-building support.
* **Tier 3 (Self-Sufficient / Graduated):** Automatic Creamy Layer graduation, competing purely on open merit.

### 5. Four Pillars of Implementation
1. **Universal Scholarship Parity**
2. **Dynamic Creamy Layer Exit**
3. **Merit & Talent Protection**
4. **Unified Yojana Delivery**
